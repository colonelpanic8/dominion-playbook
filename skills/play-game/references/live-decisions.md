# Decisions at the table

**Opening.** Compare the best two openings for the actual split. Identify what
draws, thins or attacks, and what each opening makes possible in the first
reshuffle. Use the actual Supply, Victory piles and setup effects. For an
on-gain effect, pay for the card first and count what remains in hand. Use
known opening-hand counts when supplied. If the next hand is uncertain, give
the condition that changes the buy.

Compare the two strongest openings on the actual split. Identify the standout
draw, thinning or attack, which cards collide on the first reshuffle, and
whether the deck can outscore a money plan before the game ends. Revisit the
choice when the opponent’s opening becomes visible.

**Clock.** Keep the first plan within the opening budget. With several minutes
banked, spend under a minute. With about a minute left, use at most ten seconds;
at LOW TIME, make a legal move immediately. Use the card text and any ratings
already supplied. Revisit close calls on later turns as the clock permits.
Plan a sequence once, then play its routine steps promptly. If the desired buy
is offered directly and Treasure order is immaterial, select the buy.

**Pile endings.** Before emptying the second pile in a two-player game, check
who gets the next opportunity to end it. Price their quickest third pile:
cards left, Buys or gains needed, total cost after discounts, and points gained
by each seat. Compare those requirements with what their deck has shown it
can produce. A player ahead now can lose on the points in that last pile.
