# Decisions at the table

**Opening.** Compare the two strongest openings for the actual split. Use the
Supply, Victory piles and setup effects to judge whether an engine can outscore
a money plan. Identify the standout draw, thinning or attack and what each
opening does on the first reshuffle, including any terminal collisions. When
buying a card with an on-gain effect, count what remains in hand after paying.
Use known hand counts when supplied. If the next hand is uncertain, give the
condition that changes the buy. Revisit the plan after the opponent's opening.

**Clock.** Keep the first plan within the opening budget. With several minutes
banked, spend under a minute. With about a minute left, use at most ten seconds.
At LOW TIME, make a legal move immediately. Use the card text and any ratings
already supplied. Revisit close calls on later turns as the clock permits.
Plan a sequence once, then play its routine steps promptly. If the desired buy
is offered directly and Treasure order is immaterial, select the buy.

**Ending a phase.** Before passing, check unused Actions and Buys against the
plan. Price one more play, including anything it must trash. If a recurring
effect forces you to trash, name what the next hand can afford to lose. When
fodder is scarce, spend a spare Buy on a cheap or free card if it can enter
the next shuffle and protect something better. Otherwise leave it in the Supply.

**Pile endings.** Before emptying the second pile in a two-player game, check
who gets the next opportunity to end it. For their quickest third pile, count the cards left,
Buys or gains needed, cost after discounts, and points gained by each seat. Compare those requirements with what their deck has shown it
can produce. A player ahead now can lose on the points in that last pile.
